\begin{abstract}

    Docked bike-sharing systems (BSSs) are an accessible, low-carbon mobility option that can replace short urban car trips and improve connectivity to public transport. As BSSs have evolved into large-scale services, performance increasingly depends on decisions across the system life cycle, from station placement to day-to-day operations. This thesis develops data-driven methods to support these tasks, using Barcelona and its docked BSS, \textit{Bicing}, as case studies.

    First, the thesis addresses station network design. Existing optimization approaches often fail to quantify system accessibility realistically, transparently explore trade-offs among diverse planning objectives (e.g., maximizing demand coverage and social equity), or explicitly control station layout, which is typically a by-product of site selection. This thesis presents a flexible planning framework combining modular multi-criteria scoring with directed street-network accessibility metrics adjusted for topography, and uses a genetic algorithm to optimize station layouts while explicitly controlling inter-station proximity and overall accessibility. Applied to Barcelona, it identifies near-optimal configurations for diverse scenarios and shows that altitude-adjusted accessibility helps correct service gaps in hilly districts. The framework is also used to identify new stations that complement an existing network.
    
    Turning to day-to-day operations, the thesis first characterizes \textit{Bicing}’s mobility patterns, which underpin two predictive use cases. The first one targets maximizing electric bike availability by keeping batteries charged without exact route information or in-transit battery measurements. The thesis proposes a joint forecasting framework coupling a Markov-chain mobility model with a physics-based battery model to simulate trajectories and energy states, yielding accurate battery estimates and reliable short-horizon station-level forecasts that enable proactive charging strategies.
    
    The second use case also targets increasing availability, but by reducing downtime through anticipation of bike component failures. The thesis develops predictive maintenance models for three key components (brake pads, wheel spokes, and chains) based on survival analysis and machine learning, using maintenance records, historical mobility indicators, and weather variables to estimate failure risk. The models achieve consistently strong predictive performance, and interpretability analysis shows that more intensive use shortens component lifetimes, in particular higher average speeds and electric propulsion.
    
    Overall, the thesis shows that docked BSS planning and operations can be improved with computational, data-driven decision support even under data limitations. The proposed methods enable a shift from heuristic planning and reactive management to evidence-based, anticipatory strategies that are transferable to other cities with comparable spatial and operational data.
    

\textbf{Keywords:} bike-sharing, electric mobility, spatial optimization, data-driven methods, station placement, battery prediction, predictive maintenance, Barcelona, Bicing.

\end{abstract}
