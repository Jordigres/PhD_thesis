\section{Thesis outline and contributions}
\label{sec:thesis_outline_and_contributions}

\subsection{Thesis outline}

This thesis is organized into five parts:

\textbf{Part 1: Introduction.}
This part establishes the context and motivation for applying \ac{ai} techniques to docked \ac{bss}. It defines the research objectives and provides an overview of the thesis structure and core contributions.

\textbf{Part 2: Foundations of \acp{bss}.}
This part provides the conceptual foundations of the thesis. It situates bike-sharing in the broader context of shared mobility systems and then focuses on \acp{bss}, outlining their main typologies, historical evolution, user profiles, and usage patterns. It also reviews the impacts of \acp{bss} across mobility, environmental, health, and socioeconomic dimensions, and surveys the ways in which data-driven computational methods have been applied to improve their design and operation.

\textbf{Part 3: Designing \ac{bss} station networks in Barcelona.}
This part addresses the station planning problem. It begins by introducing the city of Barcelona as the case study for this part's research, outlining its urban characteristics and existing \ac{bss} infrastructure. After reviewing the state of the art in station placement strategies, it presents a flexible framework for optimizing the spatial configuration of docking stations. The method combines a modular multi-criteria scoring system with network-based and topography-adjusted accessibility metrics, and applies a genetic algorithm to generate optimized station configurations that align with diverse planning objectives while respecting spatial and operational constraints.

\textbf{Part 4: Predictive operations in \textit{Bicing}.}  
Building on the operational data from Barcelona’s \ac{bss}, \textit{Bicing}, this part explores how predictive models can support more efficient system management. Two complementary applications are presented. First, after reviewing the state of the art in mobility modeling and battery prediction for \ac{e-b} fleets, a joint prediction framework is proposed to estimate \acp{e-b} locations and battery levels by combining a Markov-based mobility model with a physics-informed energy consumption model. Second, following a review of existing approaches to \ac{pm} and failure modeling, \ac{pm} models are developed to estimate failure risks in both \acp{m-b} and \acp{e-b} using \ac{sa} techniques while providing prediction interpretability.

\textbf{Part 5: Closing reflections.}
This final part returns to the central research questions and objectives, providing a summary of the thesis’s main results in station planning, mobility and battery modeling, and \ac{pm}. It then discusses the significance of these findings for the field of \acp{bss}, highlights the limitations of the study, suggests avenues for future research, and concludes with a concise synthesis.


\subsection{Contributions}

The main contributions of this thesis, all rooted in the context of docked \acp{bss}, are as follows.

\begin{enumerate}  
    \item A flexible and modular data-driven framework for station planning that enables fine-grained spatial analysis and supports multi-criteria evaluation of candidate sites. The method integrates network-based accessibility metrics computed on directed, topography-aware street networks to reflect real-world cycling conditions. It also supports the incremental expansion and reconfiguration of existing station networks, allowing planners to explore trade-offs between competing objectives in a realistic and adaptable way.

    \item An approach for jointly predicting \acp{e-b} locations and battery levels in \acp{bss} fleets, combining a Markov-based mobility model with a physics-informed energy consumption model.

    \item A predictive maintenance strategy for \acp{bss}, based on interpretable survival models that estimate component-level failure risks for both \acp{m-b} and \acp{e-b}.

    \item Demonstration of all proposed methods through real-world applications in Barcelona and its \ac{bss}, \textit{Bicing}, showing how \ac{ai} techniques can be operationalized to support decision-making in \acp{bss}.

\end{enumerate}
