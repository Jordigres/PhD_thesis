\chapter[Predicting Failures in BSS Maintenance Operations]{Predicting Failures in \ac{bss} Maintenance Operations}
\label{ch:failure_forecasting_bicing}

After Chapters~\ref{ch:bicing_mobility_patterns} and~\ref{ch:mobility_and_battery_modeling_bicing} document the mobility and battery dynamics of \textit{Bicing}, Part~\ref{pt:predictive_operations} turns to bike maintenance. Here, the central challenge is that \acp{m-b} and \acp{e-b} are used in different ways, and consequently, the wear and tear on the different bike components can evolve differently. Moreover, to make maintenance planning proactive rather than reactive, operators not only need component-failure risk estimates but also interpretable indicators to build trust in the predictions. This motivates the following research question: \emph{How can data-driven and interpretable predictive maintenance strategies be developed to anticipate vehicle failures, optimize maintenance scheduling, and improve the operational efficiency and sustainability of fleet services?}

To address the research question posed above, this Chapter develops the \ac{pm} contribution of the thesis for bike components. It builds on the mobility context to analyze maintenance data for brake pads, wheel spokes, and chains, documenting distinct failure dynamics between \acp{m-b} and \acp{e-b} and the role of cumulative distance and bike type. It then trains \ac{sa} and \ac{ml}-based survival models to estimate component-level failure risk, with interpretability analyses that align predictors to empirical wear patterns.

The material in this Chapter is adapted from Grau-Escolano et al.~\cite{Grau-Escolano2024}, published in \textit{EPJ Data Science}.
