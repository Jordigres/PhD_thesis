\section{Research problem}
\label{sec:conclusions_research_problem}

Over the past decade, \acp{bss} have become an increasingly important component of urban mobility as more systems are implemented and existing ones expand, making them a relevant part of how people move around cities. This growth has been accompanied by the deployment of digital infrastructure that produces rich operational data, creating new opportunities to improve both long-term system design and day-to-day operations through computational, data-driven decision support.

Considerable progress has been made in integrating data and computational methods into \ac{bss} planning and operations, including work on system design, demand modelling, and rebalancing. Nevertheless, important gaps remain. Building on prior advances, the central research problem addressed in this thesis is how \ac{bss} data can be most effectively harnessed to improve both efficiency and sustainability through computational, data-driven methodologies. This problem is operationalised in three research questions:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item How can a single, flexible station-planning framework be developed that explores multiple, competing planning objectives and is detailed enough to support practical, street-level system design?
    \item How can \ac{e-b} battery levels be predicted without exact routes or in-transit measurements, so as to improve bike availability and operational efficiency?
    \item How can data-driven and interpretable predictive maintenance strategies be developed to anticipate vehicle failures, optimize maintenance scheduling, and improve the operational efficiency and sustainability of fleet services?
\end{enumerate}
