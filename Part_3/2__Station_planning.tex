\chapter[Designing BSS Station Networks]{Designing \ac{bss} Station Networks}
\label{ch:station_planning_in_barcelona}


Building on the Barcelona case-study context presented in the previous section, this Chapter~\ref{ch:station_planning_in_barcelona} addresses one of the most consequential design decisions in docked \acp{bss}: where to place docking stations. This has a great impact because it shapes accessibility, the demand the system can attract, and the operational burden associated with rebalancing and service reliability. Existing planning frameworks are often rigid: they typically optimize only a limited set of objectives, evaluate candidate sites on coarse areal units rather than along the street network, and overlook topography. Consequently, this Chapter addresses the following research question: \emph{How can a single, flexible station-planning framework be developed that explores multiple, competing planning objectives and is detailed enough to support practical, street-level system design?}

First, the Chapter reviews the state of the art in station placement and then presents the proposed flexible optimization framework. The framework combines an \ac{mcdm} scoring layer, which evaluates candidate sites under heterogeneous and scenario-specific criteria and weights, with an optimization process solved through a \ac{ga}. Moreover, network-level distances are computed on a directed, topography-adjusted street graph. The framework is then demonstrated through three planning scenarios in Barcelona and a network expansion case study in l'Eixample district.

The material in this Chapter is adapted from Grau-Escolano et al.~\cite{GrauEscolano2025}, published in \textit{Journal of Urban Mobility}.
